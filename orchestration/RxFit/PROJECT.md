## 1. What Is RxFit?

RxFit is a premium concierge executive advisory firm operating in Austin, TX. Operating partners travel to the client — whether that's a home office, a company headquarters, a private workspace, or a partner workspace facility. There are no commutes, no waiting on steering committees, no wasted cycles. Every session is engineered around the client's schedule, business, and outcome.

RxFit operates at the intersection of white-glove service and applied operating discipline. The experience is closer to having an embedded chief of staff than a traditional consultant. Every engagement is anchored in data — operating metrics, pipeline coverage, burn multiples, cadence targets — not vibes.

The company extends its reach through **rxfit.ai**, a subscription-based AI advisory copilot that delivers personalized operating playbooks, planning guidance, and accountability infrastructure to clients who want the RxFit methodology but don't require in-person sessions.

## 3. Target Avatar — Deep Psychographic + Demographic Profile

RxFit exists for a specific person. Every piece of content, every service offering, every pricing decision is made with this avatar in mind.

### 3.1 Primary Avatar — "The Austin Achiever"

**Demographics:**
- Age: 30–50 years old
- Gender: Male or female (skews slightly male at the scale-up tier, balanced at the advisory tier)
- Income: $180K–$500K+ household
- Occupation: Senior engineering manager, management consultant, finance executive, tech founder, corporate attorney, sales VP
- Location: Austin, TX — specifically ZIP codes 78746 (West Lake Hills), 78733 (Bee Cave area), 78731 (Bull Creek / Loop 360), 78703 (Tarrytown), 78704 (South Congress / 78704 aspirational), 78730 (River Place)
- Education: Bachelor's minimum, often graduate degree or advanced certifications
- Family: Married or partnered, often with children age 5–18

**Psychographics:**
- Outcome-obsessed, not process-addicted. They don't want to "love the offsite" — they want the business to look and perform a specific way and will invest to achieve it efficiently.
- High opportunity cost of time. An hour with a traditional consultancy (scoping call + steering committee + readout deck + follow-up) costs them 2.5 hours. On-site means 60 productive minutes and back to their team or family.
- Skeptical of hype. They've been burned by fad frameworks, retainers they didn't use, and advisors who didn't push them. They need evidence — cohort retention data, operating-cadence logic, metric tracking.
- Status-adjacent but not superficial. They're not trying to be LinkedIn-famous. They want to feel excellent at 42. They want to run a board meeting in February without prep panic, be present with their kids without carrying operational strain home, and look credible walking into a boardroom.
- Anti-hustle-culture. They find the social dynamics of startup accelerators mildly hostile — loud demo days, crowded cohorts, performance anxiety. Private advisory removes all friction.
- Already buying premium everywhere else. A McKinsey retainer they barely use, a Masterclass subscription gathering dust, a Notion operating dashboard they actually check. They understand the value of curated operating infrastructure — they just need execution support.
- Data-native. They track NDR, burn multiple, pipeline coverage. They want their advisor to interpret these signals, not ignore them.

**Pain Points:**
- Inconsistent follow-through when left to self-direct
- Time compression — work, family, and strategic thinking are competing for the same hours
- Previous advisors who plateaued them or didn't track progress rigorously
- Financial operations as the missing variable — they sell well but plan runway poorly under stress
- Accountability gap — needs external structure to maintain operating discipline

**Buying Triggers:**
- A company event: crossing $1M ARR, a near-miss on payroll, a goal (Series A, acquisition, board seat)
- Social proof from a peer they respect
- Visible results testimonial from someone in their demographic
- A frictionless first touch — the brand needs to feel as premium as the service before they spend a dollar

### 3.2 Secondary Avatar — "The Rx360 Client"

- Already engaged with RxFit in-person, wants to extend into full-stack advisory
- Interested in operating-metric tracking (NDR, burn multiple), cadence optimization, and pipeline management
- Willing to invest in the full stack: strategy + operations + growth playbooks

### 3.3 Tertiary Avatar — "The rxfit.ai Subscriber"

- Lives outside Austin or travels frequently
- Wants the RxFit methodology but can self-execute with a structured operating plan
- Price-sensitive relative to in-person but still premium-market (not shopping on Groupon)
- Likely a former in-person client or a referral from one

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

**Voice Pillars:**
1. **Premium** — Never apologize for the price. Lead with value, outcome, and transformation. The price is the least interesting thing about RxFit.
2. **Data-Backed** — Reference operating cadence, pipeline coverage, burn multiple, cohort retention. Advisors are operator-credentialed (ex-founder / ex-FAANG). The methodology is not improvised.
3. **Personal** — This isn't a franchise or a tech startup with stock photos. It's Danny Trejo, Austin TX, with his name on every session. That intimacy is the product.
4. **Results-Driven** — Talk in outcomes. Not "we do strategy work" — "your operating cadence is fixed in 6 weeks or we rebuild the playbook." Specific. Verifiable.
5. **Respectful of Intelligence** — The avatar reads Stratechery, reads McKinsey reports, listens to Lex Fridman. Don't write down to them. They'll feel the condescension and leave.

**Tone Modifiers:**
- Direct, not aggressive
- Confident, not arrogant
- Warm, not casual
- Precise, not cold

**What We Never Say:**
- "Transform your business" (generic)
- "Crush Q4" (seasonal, low-stakes)
- "Affordable pricing" (anti-premium signal)
- Anything that sounds like a growth-hacking ad from 2012
