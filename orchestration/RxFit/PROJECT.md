## 1. What Is RxFit?

RxFit is a premium concierge personal training company operating in Austin, TX. Trainers travel to the client — whether that's a home gym, a building fitness center, a private outdoor space, or a satellite gym facility. There are no commutes, no waiting for equipment, no wasted reps. Every session is engineered around the client's schedule, body, and outcome.

RxFit operates at the intersection of white-glove service and applied sports science. The experience is closer to having a private performance coach than a traditional personal trainer. Every engagement is anchored in data — biometrics, recovery scores, nutrition logs, progressive overload targets — not vibes.

The company extends its reach through **rxfit.ai**, a subscription-based AI coaching platform that delivers personalized programming, nutrition guidance, and accountability infrastructure to clients who want the RxFit methodology but don't require in-person sessions.

## 3. Target Avatar — Deep Psychographic + Demographic Profile

RxFit exists for a specific person. Every piece of content, every service offering, every pricing decision is made with this avatar in mind.

### 3.1 Primary Avatar — "The Austin Achiever"

**Demographics:**
- Age: 30–50 years old
- Gender: Male or female (skews slightly male at the performance tier, balanced at wellness tier)
- Income: $180K–$500K+ household
- Occupation: Senior engineering manager, management consultant, finance executive, tech founder, corporate attorney, sales VP
- Location: Austin, TX — specifically ZIP codes 78746 (West Lake Hills), 78733 (Bee Cave area), 78731 (Bull Creek / Loop 360), 78703 (Tarrytown), 78704 (South Congress / 78704 aspirational), 78730 (River Place)
- Education: Bachelor's minimum, often graduate degree or advanced certifications
- Family: Married or partnered, often with children age 5–18

**Psychographics:**
- Outcome-obsessed, not process-addicted. They don't want to "love the gym" — they want to look and perform a specific way and will invest to achieve it efficiently.
- High opportunity cost of time. An hour at a traditional gym (commute + parking + waiting for equipment + shower + commute back) costs them 2.5 hours. In-home means 60 productive minutes and back to their desk or family.
- Skeptical of hype. They've been burned by fad diets, gym memberships they didn't use, and trainers who didn't push them. They need evidence — body composition data, periodization logic, biometric tracking.
- Status-adjacent but not superficial. They're not trying to be Instagram-fit. They want to feel excellent at 42. They want to ski hard in February, play with their kids without back pain, and look credible walking into a boardroom.
- Anti-gym-culture. They find the social dynamics of commercial gyms mildly hostile — loud music, crowded equipment, performance anxiety. Private training removes all friction.
- Already buying premium everywhere else. Equinox membership they barely use, Peloton gathering dust, Whoop or Oura they actually check. They understand the value of curated health infrastructure — they just need execution support.
- Data-native. They track sleep scores, HRV, resting heart rate. They want their trainer to interpret these signals, not ignore them.

**Pain Points:**
- Inconsistent follow-through when left to self-direct
- Time compression — work, family, and self-care are competing for the same hours
- Previous trainers who plateaued them or didn't track progress rigorously
- Nutrition as the missing variable — they train but eat poorly under stress
- Accountability gap — needs external structure to maintain discipline

**Buying Triggers:**
- A life event: turning 40, a health scare, a goal (marathon, wedding, reunion)
- Social proof from a peer they respect
- Visible results testimonial from someone in their demographic
- A frictionless first touch — the brand needs to feel as premium as the service before they spend a dollar

### 3.2 Secondary Avatar — "The Rx360 Client"

- Already training with RxFit in-person, wants to extend into full wellness
- Interested in biometric tracking (Oura, Whoop), sleep optimization, and stress management
- Willing to invest in the full stack: training + nutrition + recovery protocols
- Likely refers others within their network

### 3.3 Tertiary Avatar — "The rxfit.ai Subscriber"

- Lives outside Austin or travels frequently
- Wants the RxFit methodology but can self-execute with a structured program
- Price-sensitive relative to in-person but still premium-market (not shopping on Groupon)
- Likely a former in-person client or a referral from one

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

**Voice Pillars:**
1. **Premium** — Never apologize for the price. Lead with value, outcome, and transformation. The price is the least interesting thing about RxFit.
2. **Science-Backed** — Reference periodization, progressive overload, biometric data, recovery science. Trainers are NASM-certified. The methodology is not improvised.
3. **Personal** — This isn't a franchise or a tech startup with stock photos. It's Danny Trejo, Austin TX, with his name on every session. That intimacy is the product.
4. **Results-Driven** — Talk in outcomes. Not "we do strength training" — "your back pain disappears in 6 weeks or we rebuild the program." Specific. Verifiable.
5. **Respectful of Intelligence** — The avatar reads Huberman Lab, reads McKinsey reports, listens to Lex Fridman. Don't write down to them. They'll feel the condescension and leave.

**Tone Modifiers:**
- Direct, not aggressive
- Confident, not arrogant
- Warm, not casual
- Precise, not cold

**What We Never Say:**
- "Transform your body" (generic)
- "Get fit for summer" (seasonal, low-stakes)
- "Affordable pricing" (anti-premium signal)
- Anything that sounds like a gym ad from 2012
