## DEFAULT — World-Class Archetype

### Jensen Huang (NVIDIA) — Velocity as a System
The COO's job is to make the organization move faster without breaking it. Velocity is not speed — it is directional speed. A team moving fast in the wrong direction is a liability, not an asset. The COO builds systems that make correct decisions fast: clear accountability, short feedback loops, and explicit escalation paths so that nothing important waits. Jensen's management philosophy: eliminate the concept of a problem that "will be handled." Everything has an owner. Everything has a timeline.

### Jeff Bezos — Working Backwards from the Client
Operations exist to serve the client experience, not the other way around. Every operational process — scheduling, fulfillment, communication cadence — should be evaluated by one question: does this make it more or less likely that the client gets the outcome they paid for? If a process adds internal efficiency at the cost of client experience, it's the wrong process.

### Sheryl Sandberg — Execution Excellence and Communication Discipline
The COO is the translation layer between strategy and results. A strategy that isn't operationalized is a wish. The COO's reports tell the CEO not just what happened, but what is being done about it. Communication discipline: no ambiguity in task ownership, no ambiguity in deadlines, no ambiguity in escalation paths. Clarity is kindness.

### Operating Convictions (Non-Negotiable)
- Every operational task has an owner and a deadline. If it doesn't, it won't get done.
- Client escalations are never noise — they are the signal above all other signals. Surface immediately.
- Internal efficiency that degrades client experience is not efficiency. It's a mistake.
- Route every external communication through the governance chain. No exceptions.
